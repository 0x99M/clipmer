An email has a name, a company, an email address and a VAT number in it, and all four
have to go into a form. The usual way is four round trips: switch to the email, find the
next piece, copy it, switch back, find the field, paste. Linux pastes only the last thing
you copied, so you can't collect all four first.

You can, with two different tools: one already built in, which holds two things, and a
clipboard history, which holds as many as you like. Working this way saves **about five
hours a year** for someone who does it a few times a day; the estimate is worked through
below.

## Why Linux pastes only the last thing you copied

**Ctrl + C** writes to the *clipboard*, and there is one of it. The next copy replaces the
last, and nothing on a stock Ubuntu, Fedora or Mint install keeps the old one. Windows has
**Win + V** for this; on those Linux desktops it is a separate tool you install.

There is, however, a second slot most people never use.

## The built-in trick: two things at once

Linux has a **primary selection** alongside the clipboard. Highlighting text with the mouse
writes to it, and a **middle-click** pastes it. The two are independent, so you can carry
two things at once:

1. Select the first piece and press **Ctrl + C**. It goes to the clipboard.
2. Highlight the second piece. Don't copy it; highlighting is enough.
3. In the form, **Ctrl + V** pastes the first, and **middle-click** pastes the second.

This works on GNOME, KDE Plasma and the wlroots compositors, on Wayland and X11, in most
applications. On a touchpad, a three-finger tap usually acts as the middle button.

It stops at two. And the primary selection is fragile: highlighting *anything* replaces
it, including selecting the contents of a form field before you paste. For more than two
pieces, you need a history.

## More than two: copy everything first

With a clipboard history every copy is kept, so the workflow turns around: collect all the
pieces from the source in one pass, then fill the whole form in one pass.

**Step 1, in the email.** Select the name, **Ctrl + C**. Select the company, **Ctrl + C**.
The email address, the VAT number: the same. You never leave the email, so you never lose
your place in it.

**Step 2, in the form.** Click the first field, open the history, pick the piece, and
repeat for each field. You never go back to the email.

Clipmer is ours, so weigh this section accordingly. With it, step 2 is three keys per
field:

1. **Ctrl + Shift + D** opens the history over the form.
2. **Type a few letters** of the piece you want — `maya`, `labs`, `@`, `SE55`. Search
   matches anywhere in an entry, ignoring case.
3. **Enter** puts it back on the clipboard and closes the window. With **Auto-paste on
   select** on, which works on GNOME, it also pastes into the field; elsewhere, Ctrl+V.

The piece you just used moves to the top of the list, so the history stays in the order
you actually use things. The free version keeps your last 100 copies, far more than any
form needs.

## The estimate

Each piece of information moved the old way costs a switch to the source, finding your
place in it again, a copy, a switch back and finding the field. Collecting first removes
the switch and the re-finding for every piece but the first, and opening the history and
typing a few letters costs a little of that back. The net saving is taken as **4 seconds
a piece**. A working year is **230 days**.

This is an estimate, not a measurement. The clock time is also the smaller part of the
gain: every switch back to the source is a chance to lose your train of thought, or to
paste the thing you copied before last. Collecting first removes those chances too, and
the table doesn't count them.

## Where else it fits

The same pattern works anywhere the pieces come from one place and go to another:

- **Writing an update from several tabs.** A link from one, a number from another, a quote
  from the third, then write the message once and paste each piece where it belongs.
- **Filing a bug report.** The error message, your system details and the URL, collected
  while you reproduce the bug, pasted when you file it.
- **Moving a contact or an order between two systems** that don't talk to each other.
