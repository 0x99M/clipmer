KDE Plasma is the one major Linux desktop that ships a clipboard history switched on. It
is the **Clipboard** icon in the system tray, **Meta + V** opens it at the mouse pointer,
and underneath it is Klipper, which has been part of KDE for over two decades.

So when it misbehaves — only ever one entry, history gone after a reboot, a paste that
suddenly produces nothing — the fix is nearly always a setting or an environment variable,
not a replacement. This page goes through the causes in order of how often they come up,
and only then gets to when a different clipboard manager makes sense. We make one of
those, and we would still check these first.

## Open the right settings

Right-click the **Clipboard** icon in the system tray and choose **Configure Clipboard…**.
If there is no icon, the widget has been removed: right-click the tray's arrow, choose
**Configure System Tray…**, and set **Clipboard** under **Entries** back to shown. Without
the widget, Plasma keeps no history at all — and nothing keeps your clipboard alive when
you close the app you copied from either.

## Only one entry ever shows up

This is the one that sends people looking for alternatives, and it has a specific cause.
In [KDE bug 500927](https://bugs.kde.org/show_bug.cgi?id=500927), the history held exactly
one item regardless of its configured size — and only when the `QT_QPA_PLATFORM`
environment variable was set to `wayland-egl`. With `wayland`, or with it unset, the
history worked normally.

Some distributions had set `wayland-egl` globally because Qt's documentation describes it
as the preferred backend. KDE closed the bug as not a bug and added a warning against it to
its packaging recommendations. Check yours:

```bash
echo $QT_QPA_PLATFORM
```

If it prints `wayland-egl`, find where it's set — usually `/etc/environment`, a file in
`~/.config/environment.d/`, or a script in `~/.config/plasma-workspace/env/` — and remove
it, or change it to `wayland`. Log out and back in.

## History is gone after a reboot

Make sure **Save history across desktop sessions** is on. With it off, Klipper keeps the
history in memory for the session only, which some people choose deliberately: a
persistent history is also a persistent record of every password you have copied.

If it is on and the history still disappears, look at whether something is clearing it.
Session-cleanup scripts and "clear history on logout" options in privacy tools are the
usual suspects.

## Paste stops working after cancelling Meta + V

A [report on KDE's forum](https://discuss.kde.org/t/plasma-6-3-4-meta-v-causes-current-copied-data-to-be-lost/32535)
from Plasma 6.3.4 describes this: open the popup with Meta + V, dismiss it by clicking
outside, and after a few rounds Ctrl+V pastes nothing in Qt or GTK apps until you choose a
different entry. Nobody in that thread identified a cause, and no bug report is linked.
If it happens to you, choosing any other entry — or copying something new — restores
pasting, and an up-to-date Plasma is the first thing to try.

## One thing Klipper does that most don't

Password managers mark the passwords they copy with a hint, `x-kde-passwordManagerHint`,
and Klipper [leaves those entries out of its history](https://phabricator.kde.org/R120:637cd0bf62e419b8038cb9b1565089fbd341ee7a).
KeePassXC sets the hint, so a password copied from it never lands in Klipper's list. That
is a genuinely good default, and one many clipboard managers — ours included — don't have
yet.

## When an alternative makes sense

Fixing Klipper is the right answer for most people. There are three situations where
something else fits better:

- **You share your screen.** Klipper shows every entry in plain text whenever you open it.
  If you open your clipboard on a call, everything you copied today is on screen.
- **You want to keep and annotate things.** A command you reuse, with a note saying which
  server it's for.
- **You use GNOME on another machine** and want one tool and one set of habits on both.

Clipmer is ours, so weigh this accordingly. It lets you **Hide** any entry from its ⋯ menu,
after which it renders as dots in the list, is left out of search, and opens masked in the
full-content viewer until you press Reveal. Folders and notes are part of Pro ($9 once);
hiding is free.

What to know before running it on Plasma:

- **Pick a shortcut other than Meta + V.** Klipper already has it. Clipmer's default is
  **Ctrl + Shift + D**, and you can change it in **Settings → Shortcuts → Toggle window**.
- **A new shortcut works after your next login.** Since 3.4.0, Clipmer writes its shortcut
  into Plasma's own configuration, which Plasma reads at login.
- **Run 3.5.0 or later.** Earlier versions could show "A JavaScript error occurred in the
  main process" when you changed the shortcut on Plasma.
- **No auto-paste.** Clipmer's paste-on-select uses a GNOME Shell helper, so on Plasma you
  choose an entry and press Ctrl+V yourself.
- **It doesn't yet skip password-manager entries.** Klipper does. If you run both, Clipmer
  records what Klipper deliberately leaves out, so hide those entries or clear them.

<Callout type="note" title="Running both">
  Klipper and Clipmer can run at the same time, each keeping its own history of the same
  copies, so trying Clipmer doesn't mean switching Klipper off.
</Callout>

We build and test Clipmer on Ubuntu with GNOME, and it runs on Plasma. If Plasma is your
only desktop and you don't share your screen, a working Klipper is probably all you need.
