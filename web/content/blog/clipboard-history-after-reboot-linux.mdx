You installed a clipboard manager precisely so you could get things back, then rebooted,
and the history is empty. Everything you copied yesterday is gone.

Sometimes that's a bug. More often it's a setting doing exactly what it was designed to
do, because forgetting on restart is a privacy feature in a lot of clipboard tools — the
one in Windows included. This page covers why some histories reset, how to make the common
Linux ones keep theirs, and what it costs you when they do.

## Forgetting is often the default

A clipboard history collects passwords, tokens and personal details whether you meant it
to or not. Keeping it only in memory means a reboot wipes all of that, which is a
reasonable trade for some people — and the reason several tools make it the default, or
offer it prominently:

- **Windows** clears its clipboard history
  [every time you restart](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/clipboard-in-windows-c436501e-985d-1c8d-97ea-fe46ddf338c6),
  keeping only pinned items.
- **The GNOME Clipboard History extension** has an *Only save favorites to disk* option that,
  in [its README's words](https://github.com/SUPERCILEX/gnome-clipboard-history/blob/master/README.md),
  wipes your non-favorited items on shutdown.
- **KDE's Klipper** has a switch for exactly this, covered below.

So before assuming something broke, check whether it was told to forget.

## Make your clipboard manager keep it

**Klipper:** right-click the Clipboard icon in the system tray, choose **Configure
Clipboard…**, and turn on **Save history across desktop sessions**. With it off, the history
lasts for one session.

**Clipboard History extension:** open its preferences in the Extension Manager app and turn
off **Only save favorites to disk** if you want everything kept, not just favourites.

**GPaste:** history is saved to disk unless `save-history` has been turned off. If the
setting is `true` and the history still comes back empty, GPaste's issue tracker has a
[report](https://github.com/Keruspe/GPaste/issues/354) of it reading from a different
directory than it wrote to — look in `~/.local/share/` for both a `GPaste` and a `gpaste`
directory.

## Or it isn't running yet

The other common cause isn't storage at all. A clipboard manager only records copies while
it's running. If it doesn't start at login, the history isn't lost after a reboot — it just
isn't there until you open the app, and nothing you copied before that was recorded.

Check that yours starts automatically. On GNOME, autostart entries live in
`~/.config/autostart/`; on Plasma, under **System Settings → Autostart**.

## How Clipmer keeps your history

Clipmer is ours, so weigh this section accordingly. It has no in-memory-only mode: every
entry is written to disk, so a reboot, a crash or a logout doesn't cost you the history.

<InstallCommand />

- **Where it lives:** `~/.config/clipmer/clipmer-data.json`, alongside your settings.
- **When it's written:** a fraction of a second after each copy, and once more when you
  quit, so at most the last moment before a hard power-off can be lost.
- **Hiding is saved immediately.** When you hide an entry, that's written straight away
  rather than batched with the next copy — an entry you just masked mustn't come back
  unmasked after a crash.
- **Starting at login is off until you turn it on**, in **Settings → General → Start on
  login**. Do that once, or Clipmer only starts recording when you first open it after each
  boot. It works for the AppImage too since 3.2.0, which earlier showed the setting as on
  while doing nothing.

The free version shows your last 100 entries; Pro keeps 200, and entries filed in folders
are kept beyond that limit.

## Moving to a new machine

A history that survives reboots should survive a reinstall too. There are two ways with
Clipmer.

**Copy the file.** Quit Clipmer from the tray menu on both machines, copy
`~/.config/clipmer/clipmer-data.json` across, and start it again. This is free and works.
Know what you are carrying, though: the file is plain JSON, it holds every entry in plain
text — hidden ones included — and it also holds your settings and your licence key if you
have one.

**Use an encrypted backup (Pro).** **Settings → License → Backup** writes your history,
notes and folders into a single file encrypted with a password you choose (the key is
derived with scrypt; the contents are AES-256-GCM). On the new machine you import it and
choose whether to **merge** it into the existing history or **replace** it; a merge
recognises entries you already have, so importing twice changes nothing. The licence key
is deliberately left out of the backup, so a copy on a USB stick or in cloud storage can't
activate Pro for anybody else.

<Callout type="warn" title="Persistence cuts both ways">
  A history that survives a reboot also keeps the password you copied three weeks ago.
  Hide sensitive entries so they don't show on screen, delete the ones you don't need, and
  clear the history now and then — [screen sharing with a clipboard manager](/blog/hide-clipboard-secrets-screen-sharing)
  covers what each of those does and doesn't protect.
</Callout>
