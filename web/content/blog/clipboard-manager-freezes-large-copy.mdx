You copy a log file, a minified bundle, a database dump or the contents of an SVG, and a
few seconds later your clipboard manager is unresponsive. Sometimes the whole desktop
stutters with it. Restarting it helps until the next copy, because the giant entry is
still in its history, and now every copy has to drag it along.

This isn't one bad app. It's a pattern with a single cause, and it shows up in the bug
trackers of tools built in completely different ways:

The [clipse report](https://github.com/savedra1/clipse/issues/369) is the clearest
diagnosis: the entire 18 MB payload went into `clipboard_history.json`, and clipse parsed,
appended to and rewrote that whole file on every new copy. The Klipper reports —
[399096](https://bugs.kde.org/show_bug.cgi?id=399096) and
[237308](https://bugs.kde.org/show_bug.cgi?id=237308) — are older and differ in detail, but
the shape is the same.

## Why a big copy hurts long after you made it

A clipboard manager does three things with every entry, and each one scales with the size
of the biggest thing in the history, not the thing you just copied:

1. **It stores it.** Most managers keep the history as one file. If that file is rewritten
   on every copy, a 20 MB entry makes *every subsequent copy* a 20 MB write.
2. **It loads it.** Opening the history means reading entries into memory and, often,
   building a preview of each one.
3. **It renders it.** A list that lays out the full text of every entry has to lay out the
   20 MB one too.

The copy that caused it happened once. The cost is paid on every copy until that entry
leaves the history — which, for an entry you never use again, can be weeks.

## Getting out of it now

- **Delete the entry.** Most clipboard managers let you remove one entry. If the window
  won't open, clearing the history from the tray or the command line works too.
- **For clipse,** the reporter removed the entry from `~/.config/clipse/clipboard_history.json`
  with `jq` and restarted the listener.
- **For Klipper,** right-click the Clipboard icon in the tray and clear the history.

## How Clipmer keeps it from happening

Clipmer is ours, so weigh this section accordingly. Its history is also a single JSON
file, which is exactly the design that bit clipse — so it guards the size of what goes in,
rather than hoping nobody copies anything big.

<InstallCommand />

- **Each entry is capped at 262,144 characters** — roughly 256 KB of text. That is far
  beyond any real snippet, command or address, and small enough that rewriting the whole
  history stays cheap. Anything longer is stored up to the cap, and the full-content viewer
  labels it **truncated**, rather than presenting part of something as if it were the
  whole thing.
- **The list shows 25 entries at a time**, with a row to load more, so opening the history
  doesn't lay out the whole thing. Previews are the first 200 characters.
- **Text only.** Image history was removed in 3.0.4; it was the largest source of memory
  pressure in the app.

<Callout type="note" title="The trade-off">
  If you copy a 2 MB log and pick it from the history later, you get its first 256 KB.
  The live clipboard isn't touched by the cap: until you copy something else, Ctrl+V still
  pastes the whole thing. For comparison, Windows' built-in clipboard history caps items at
  4 MB.
</Callout>

## Why a size cap is the right fix

The clipse thread arrived at the same answer: its suggested fix is a cap of around 2 MB,
with oversized entries truncated or ignored. A clipboard history exists to give you back
the things you copy *again* — commands, URLs, addresses, replies. Nobody re-pastes a 20 MB
SVG from their history; if you need it twice, it belongs in a file.

So a cap gives up almost nothing and protects everything else. The thing to check in any
clipboard manager is whether it has one at all, and whether it tells you when it applied
it. CopyQ added per-MIME size limits in 16.0.0, 100 MiB per format by default;
[CopyQ alternatives on Linux](/blog/copyq-alternatives) has the detail alongside what else
it stores. Silent truncation is its own problem: a key or certificate cut short without a
word is worse than one that wasn't saved.

## If it isn't the clipboard manager

Two things can look like this without being it:

- **The app you copied from.** Some applications freeze while *producing* a large copy,
  because they render it in several formats at once. If the stall happens at Ctrl+C rather
  than afterwards, it's the source app.
- **Large images on GNOME.** Mutter keeps its own copy of the latest clipboard item so it
  survives closing the source app, up to 200 MB for an image. A
  [Mutter issue](https://gitlab.gnome.org/GNOME/mutter/-/issues/2818) describes a short
  freeze when copying a large image — that's the compositor, not your clipboard manager.
