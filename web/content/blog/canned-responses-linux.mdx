If you answer customers, students or colleagues, a third of what you write is something
you've written before: how to reset a password, where the refund policy is, the steps to
import old data. Each time, you either retype it or find the last time you sent it and
copy it from there. Keeping those replies one shortcut away saves **about 20 hours a
year** for someone handling 30 conversations a day. The arithmetic is below.

## Use your helpdesk's macros first

If all your replies go out through one helpdesk — Zendesk, Freshdesk, Help Scout,
Intercom — it almost certainly has saved replies built in, often called *macros*. They
can fill in the customer's name and set the ticket status as well, which no clipboard tool
can. Use them.

The gap is everywhere else. The same answer also goes into email, a team chat, a GitHub
issue, a forum thread, a sales call's follow-up. Macros live inside one tool; a clipboard
history works in all of them.

## Saved replies with a clipboard history

A clipboard history already holds every reply you've copied. Making it a reply library
takes one change of habit: when you write a good answer, copy it once. From then on it's
in the history and searchable.

Clipmer is ours, so weigh this section accordingly.

Sending a saved reply is three keys:

1. Click into the reply box and press **Ctrl + Shift + D**.
2. **Type a word from the reply** — `refund`, `import`, `reset`. Search covers the whole
   text, not only the first line, so a word from the middle of a long answer finds it.
3. **Enter**. With **Auto-paste on select** on, which works on GNOME, the reply is pasted
   into the box; elsewhere you press Ctrl+V.

Using a reply moves it to the top of the list, so the answers you give most stay nearest.
Multi-line replies keep their line breaks.

## Keeping replies for good

The free version keeps your last 100 copies. A reply you use every day never falls off,
because each use moves it back to the top. A reply you use once a month does: a hundred
copies later, it's gone.

Clipmer Pro, a one-time $9, keeps them permanently:

- **Folders.** File a reply from its **⋯** menu with **Add to folder** — a *Replies*
  folder, or one per product. Filed entries are exempt from the history limit and from
  **Clear history**.
- **Folder tabs from the keyboard.** In the open window, **→** and **←** switch folders,
  so a saved reply is **Ctrl + Shift + D**, **→**, **↓**, **Enter**.
- **Notes.** Add a note such as *refund, EU customers* to an entry, and press **Tab** in
  the search box to search notes instead of content. Useful for replies that don't
  contain the word you'd search for.

## The estimate

Finding a previous reply and copying it, or retyping it, takes 30 seconds or more; picking
it from the history takes about 5. The saving is taken as **30 seconds a reply**, and a
working year as **230 days**.

These are estimates, not measurements: one in three conversations using a standard answer
is an assumption, and your ratio may be higher or lower. Retyping also costs more than
time — the copy-pasted answer is the one you already checked for typos and wrong links.

## Starting a library in a week

You don't need to write a library up front. For one week, every time you write an answer
you'll probably give again, copy it once after sending. By Friday the answers you give
most are in the history, ranked by how often you use them. File the ones worth keeping,
and delete the drafts.

If you also move pieces of information from one place to another — a customer's details
from an email into a form, say — [copying several things at once](/blog/copy-multiple-items-linux)
covers that.
